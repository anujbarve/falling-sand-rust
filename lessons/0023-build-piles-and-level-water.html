Build piles and level water

Create sand piles and a bounded water-leveling rule while preventing directional bias.

Previously: Prevent a grain moving twice per tick. Keep that working result available before starting this lesson.

Why we need this now

Falling straight down cannot build a sloped pile or spread a pool. We need alternative destinations when the cell below is blocked. Powder and liquid movement share occupancy queries but have different fallback rules, so material kind must guide the candidate search.

Your result: Create sand piles and a bounded water-leveling rule while preventing directional bias.

01Start from the blocked downward case

First attempt the approved downward path. If it is blocked, sand may try downward diagonals subject to its repose rule; water may also search sideways through free or eligible slots. Candidate destinations are not permission to teleport through intervening walls. Every larger move must inspect its path.

Build a tiny stone bowl with one narrow column of sand or water above it. This fixture is more informative than an infinite flat floor because it creates blocked, diagonal, sideways and contained cases close together. Use the same fixture for before/after counts so leaks are visible.

02Distinguish powder slopes from liquid leveling

A powder pile needs a stability criterion, not endless diagonal drifting. The game design names a repose-angle rule, which should be approved and tested at cell scale. Water aims to flatten within its connected basin, but the initial bounded sideways search is an approximation rather than a pressure solver.

Do not claim a two-candidate toy rule already implements realistic fluid mechanics. It is a small readable rule that produces the game’s desired visible behavior. Write down the maximum search distance and the blocked-path condition. Those bounds become part of the later thread footprint proof and performance budget.

03Remove a permanent left-first preference

If every cell tries left before right, symmetric scenes can develop a persistent left bias. Alternate preference deterministically using step count or an approved coordinate hash. Deterministic alternation gives varied choices without depending on thread timing or a global RNG consumed in an unstable order.

The focused helper below chooses between two known-eligible candidates. It is not the entire liquid rule. Its job is to expose the tie-break policy: when both exist, choose according to the current parity; when only one exists, choose it; when neither exists, report no move.

04Judge the complete pile and pool contracts

Integrate the candidate selector into the world’s checked movement function and retain the moved marker. Test a symmetric sand pour, a contained water basin, a stone partition and an active-boundary wall. Count material after many steps and compare mirror fixtures to reveal systematic bias.

The rendering frame should show the latest state independently of movement frequency. If the pool behaves differently merely because the camera redraws faster, return to the tick scheduler rather than retuning sideways reach. Storage, scheduling and material policy should remain separate causes you can inspect.

Walk through the working example

Complete focused example — src/main.rs

Download this worked example ↧

fn choose_side(left:Option<usize>,right:Option<usize>,tick:u64)->Option<usize> {
    let (first,second)=if tick%2==0 {(left,right)} else {(right,left)};
    first.or(second)
}
fn main() {
    assert_eq!(choose_side(Some(3),Some(5),0),Some(3));
    assert_eq!(choose_side(Some(3),Some(5),1),Some(5));
    assert_eq!(choose_side(None,Some(5),0),Some(5));
    assert_eq!(choose_side(None,None,1),None);
    println!("deterministic tie-break verified");
}

Read what each part does

  1. This is a complete helper fixture, not a full fluid implementation.
  2. Option represents the absence of an eligible candidate.
  3. Material eligibility and candidate selection remain separate contracts.

Expected result

deterministic tie-break verified. Candidates must already be validated by occupancy and path rules before this selector is used.

Exercises you actually do

Write a prediction or make an attempt before opening a solution. Use the hint if stuck; then compare reasoning and verify with the stated check. A different implementation is fine when it satisfies the same contract.

01

Mirror the fixture

Pour equal sand into mirrored small bowls and compare settled counts and shapes.

Need a hint?

A directional preference may survive unnoticed in one scene.

Worked solution

Use identical inputs and mirrored geometry, then inspect persistent skew over several pours. Deterministic alternation is a baseline; if it is still biased, improve the rule through recorded fixtures rather than unseeded randomness.

How to verify your result

The comparison names the preference policy and records any remaining asymmetry.

02

Block the path

Put stone between water and a distant empty candidate.

Need a hint?

An empty endpoint does not prove an open route.

Worked solution

The search must stop at the blocking cell. Add a test where a naive endpoint-only move would cross the partition and assert that no water appears beyond it.

How to verify your result

No material crosses the stone partition.

03

Keep both counts

Run the water basin for hundreds of ticks and count water and stone.

Need a hint?

Leveling should transfer occupancy, not create fluid.

Worked solution

In a closed no-source/no-sink fixture both counts remain constant. A leveling image alone is insufficient because duplicated water can also look flat.

How to verify your result

The test verifies shape behavior and conservation separately.

When it goes wrong

Start with the observed symptom. Change one assumption at a time instead of pasting fixes until the error disappears.

Piles slide left forever

What is happening: Tie-breaking always favors one direction or stability is undefined.

Try this: Alternate choices deterministically and enforce the approved repose rule.

Water passes through a wall

What is happening: Only a distant endpoint was checked.

Try this: Inspect every traversed cell and stop at the first blocker.

Water never spreads

What is happening: Only downward/diagonal powder rules were implemented.

Try this: Add the bounded liquid fallback explicitly and test a blocked-downward pool.

Explain it without the example

Why alternate the horizontal preference instead of always trying left first?

Reasoning to compare

A fixed preference creates directional bias. A deterministic alternation reduces that bias while keeping runs reproducible.
