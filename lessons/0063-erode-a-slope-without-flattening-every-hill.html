Erode a slope without flattening every hill

Channels become visible at the required deadline while the hill retains its broad shape over the longer fixture.

Previously: Age wet wood and rust metal. Keep that working result available before starting this lesson.

Why we need this now

Rain should carve a channel, but the same rule repeated for years should not erase every hillside. Erosion is a spatial transfer with two time-scale promises: enough change to notice soon, and enough stability to keep a recognizable world later. Both must guide tuning.

Your result: Channels become visible at the required deadline while the hill retains its broad shape over the longer fixture.

01Move material rather than simply deleting it

An eligible exposed loose cell checks a downhill destination and moves there under the bounded rule. That transfer changes the surface heightmap at source and destination. In a closed fixture, the number of Soil/Sand/Ash/Rust cells should remain constant unless a separately specified conversion occurs. Deleting the source and never depositing it can create a convincing channel in a screenshot while silently draining the landscape. The test should count matter as well as shape change.

02Define eligibility and direction explicitly

The design uses sky exposure, slope and rain or soaked-from-rain state. Derive slope from neighbouring settled surface heights rather than from screen shading. Tie-break left and right deterministically. Keep a local move bound and validate the entire path so erosion does not tunnel through a stone dam. Stone erodes at a much smaller rate and may need its own threshold/progress policy. Do not reuse rapid sand falling as the erosion rate; they solve different timescales.

03Stage neighbouring edits carefully

Two sampled cells may select the same destination. Use a fixed deterministic application order or reserve targets in a staged edit pass. If a source moves downhill into a later selected cell, the once-per-tick guard should keep it from eroding twice. Update exposure metadata after committed changes, not from an intermediate half-edited world that another worker can read unpredictably. This is a good reason to keep slow erosion edits serial or staged while the basic rule is being established.

04Tune with short and long fixtures

Measure a bare-soil slope under scripted rain for one in-game day at 16x and record a channel profile. Then run many years under the declared weather schedule and compare broad crest/slope metrics. A channel can deepen locally while the overall hill retains its shape. The exact acceptable metrics are a design decision, but they must be written before judging the result. Use a dry control and a stone-rich control so the test can distinguish erosion from gravity instability in generated terrain.

fn downhill(left_height:i32, current_height:i32, right_height:i32)->Option<i32> {
    if left_height < current_height && left_height <= right_height { Some(-1) }
    else if right_height < current_height { Some(1) }
    else { None }
}
#[test]
fn direction_requires_a_lower_neighbour() {
    assert_eq!(downhill(4,5,7),Some(-1));
    assert_eq!(downhill(7,5,3),Some(1));
    assert_eq!(downhill(5,5,5),None);
}

01

Find the bias

For left=4,current=5,right=4, which way does this lab choose?

Need a hint?

Worked solution

It chooses left. Add this case, then pass a deterministic preference or alternate the tie decision to prevent systematic landscape drift in production.

02

Conserve a channel

Which two writes does moving one soil cell require?

Need a hint?

Source and destination must be updated together.

Worked solution

Clear or swap the source and place the same material at the validated destination. Count Soil before/after and check that the two coordinates change as a pair.

How to verify your result

The channel fixture preserves soil count in the closed region.

03

Compare years

What would make a one-day erosion success fail the long-run requirement?

Need a hint?

Readability soon and stability later are different outcomes.

Worked solution

If the same rate flattens the hill after a few years, the long-run requirement fails despite a nice first-day channel. Adjust qualified frequency/rate or slope rules and rerun both fixtures.

How to verify your result

The report includes both one-day and many-year profiles.

When it goes wrong

Start with the observed symptom. Change one assumption at a time instead of pasting fixes until the error disappears.

Soil vanishes during rain

What is happening: The rule erases instead of deposits or the destination is overwritten later.

Try this: Track source/destination transactions and counts.

Terrain always drifts left

What is happening: Equal choices use fixed left preference.

Try this: Use a tested deterministic tie-break.

The whole slope collapses before rain

What is happening: Initial terrain violates resting slope constraints.

Try this: Separate gravity stability from weather erosion before tuning rates.

Explain it without the example

Why does a good one-day erosion clip not establish a good long-term rate?

Reasoning to compare

A process that looks readable quickly may destroy terrain after many repetitions. Short-term visibility and long-term stability are separate constraints.
