Apply precipitation at exposed surfaces

Sheltered interiors stay dry, rain extinguishes fire within two seconds, and saturated surfaces create water without overwriting solids.

Previously: Generate weather from position and time. Keep that working result available before starting this lesson.

Why we need this now

The rendered rain streak is an atmospheric cue. The simulation still needs a precise answer to “what did this rain hit?” The current design applies precipitation at exposed world surfaces, which avoids spawning a dense column of falling water blocks and prevents the camera height from changing travel time.

Your result: Sheltered interiors stay dry, rain extinguishes fire within two seconds, and saturated surfaces create water without overwriting solids.

01Resolve the receiver in world coordinates

For a column, the settled terrain heightmap gives the structural surface. Attached plants or burning material above that surface can be the actual precipitation receiver under the current rule. Query that receiver before applying water or snow. Do not use the top of the screen as a physical cloud or invent a hit inside an underground camera view. If the receiver lies outside the active region, the frozen-material policy prevents mutating it just because the weather baseline can be evaluated there.

02One hit has one accounted outcome

A porous receiver absorbs until its capacity is reached. A saturated or nonporous receiver creates runoff in valid adjacent air according to the chosen placement rule; water must not overwrite stone or a roof. Plants absorb or drip under their own documented handling. A burning receiver first follows the extinguishing/wetting policy. Keep this as one simulation event so a decorative streak crossing the same column cannot apply another hit and double the rain rate.

03Choose rain versus snow from surface climate

The snow decision belongs to ambient temperature at the world surface, not at the top of the current view. Otherwise panning upward could make the same hillside snow while panning down makes it rain. The exact threshold is part of the climate rule. The lab below uses temperature below zero for snow and nonnegative temperature for rain. For production, combine it with the actual weather field and light/medium/heavy hit probability.

04Build shelter fixtures before beautiful streaks

Use one small scene with exposed soil, a roof over dry material, a leaf canopy, open water and an underground cavity. Apply the same scripted hits and assert which cells change. The roof receiver can become wet while the interior remains dry. Once those tests pass, draw streaks clipped at the same shelter boundary. If the visual treatment deliberately differs, label and approve that difference rather than allowing the renderer to silently choose a new simulation rule.

#[derive(Debug, PartialEq)]
enum Hit { Rain, Snow }
fn precipitation(surface_temperature: i16, sky_exposed: bool,
                 active: bool) -> Option<Hit> {
    if !sky_exposed || !active { return None; }
    Some(if surface_temperature < 0 { Hit::Snow } else { Hit::Rain })
}
#[test]
fn surface_climate_and_shelter_decide() {
    assert_eq!(precipitation(-2,true,true), Some(Hit::Snow));
    assert_eq!(precipitation(2,true,true), Some(Hit::Rain));
    assert_eq!(precipitation(2,false,true), None);
    assert_eq!(precipitation(2,true,false), None);
}

Exercises you actually do

01

Keep the camera out

Would a view-top temperature argument be sufficient?

Need a hint?

The surface is a fixed place in the world.

Worked solution

No. Use ambient at the receiver’s surface altitude. A view-top sample changes when the camera pans and may select the wrong precipitation kind.

How to verify your result

Two camera heights produce the same hit kind at one surface.

02

Avoid overwriting a roof

A saturated roof needs runoff but the cell below is solid. Where may new water go?

Need a hint?

A placement rule must inspect candidate occupancy.

Worked solution

Choose an approved free cell above or beside the receiving surface through a bounded search. If no slot is valid, retain or account for the hit using the declared policy; never overwrite solid material silently.

How to verify your result

A full-space fixture does not destroy roof cells.

03

Check extinguishing time

How many 1x ticks are allowed by the two-second rain/fire deadline?

Need a hint?

Base tick rate is 20 per second.

Worked solution

The limit is 40 ticks. Script sufficient rain hits and observe an active fire. Separate failure to generate hits from failure of the extinguishing reaction.

How to verify your result

The fixture records the first extinguished tick at or before 40.

When it goes wrong

Start with the observed symptom. Change one assumption at a time instead of pasting fixes until the error disappears.

It rains inside a cave

What is happening: Receiver lookup uses screen columns without shelter.

Try this: Resolve sky exposure in world coordinates before applying hits.

Water volume grows with graphics quality

What is happening: Decorative streak count also drives simulation hits.

Try this: Decouple rendering intensity from the accounted hit stream.

Snow changes when the camera moves

What is happening: Kind selection reads spawn/view height.

Try this: Use surface ambient temperature instead.

Explain it without the example

Why should decorative rain streaks not add a second moisture update?

Reasoning to compare

Simulation precipitation already accounts for water and wetness. Letting the decoration also apply it doubles the effect and couples physics to rendering.
