Melt snow in warm air

Warm snow becomes water within ten seconds at 1x. Cold snow remains snow under the fixture, and conversion clears stale attachment or movement flags as specified.

Previously: Freeze and melt water without losing cells. Keep that working result available before starting this lesson.

Why we need this now

Snow arrives as loose powder, so it must settle and then become flowing water when warm. If snow is only a weather drawing, a roof never gains weight and melting never feeds a puddle. We already have powder movement and water behavior; the new piece is an explicit bridge between those existing states.

Your result: Warm snow becomes water within ten seconds at 1x. Cold snow remains snow under the fixture, and conversion clears stale attachment or movement flags as specified.

01Reuse behavior rather than duplicate water

Give Snow a powder kind and its own temperature. Before melting, movement follows the light-powder rule, including wind where enabled. After melting, the resulting Water uses the existing liquid rules. There should not be a second “meltwater engine.” This is why material transitions are powerful: a small local state change can reveal behavior already implemented elsewhere. Test the output identity and let the next simulation pass move it according to the documented update marker.

02Initialize creation paths consistently

Painted snow and weather snow originate in different code. The paint path starts at its paint temperature, while weather chooses precipitation from surface ambient conditions. Both must create a valid Snow cell with the right powder flags and no leftover burning/rooted state. Use a named constructor or transition helper for shared invariants. Keep the origin-specific temperature explicit. A test should create snow through each path, warm it, and run the same conversion rule on both.

03Measure the ten-second promise

At a base rate of 20 executed ticks per real second, ten seconds provides 200 ticks at 1x. That is a visibility deadline, not permission to wait exactly 200 ticks. Establish a fixture with snow touching warm air, measure when the first cell changes to water, and check the limit. If the heat model takes longer, tune its approved coefficients rather than secretly accelerating the test clock. Retain a cold control so unconditional melting cannot pass.

04Decide what a melted cell retains

Water should occupy the same grid slot immediately after the change unless movement occurs through the ordinary pass. This keeps volume accounting simple: one Snow cell becomes one Water cell in this abstraction. A snow-specific attachment, wind-delay counter or colour variant must not prevent the new liquid from flowing. For the small lab, store material and temperature only. In the full Cell record, list fields retained, cleared and recomputed so the transition remains deliberate as the engine grows.

#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq)]
enum Material { Snow, Water }
#[derive(Clone, Copy)]
struct Cell { material: Material, temperature: i16 }
fn melt(cell: &mut Cell) {
    if cell.material == Material::Snow && cell.temperature > 0 {
        cell.material = Material::Water;
    }
}
#[test]
fn warm_snow_melts_but_cold_snow_does_not() {
    let mut warm = Cell { material: Material::Snow, temperature: 2 };
    let mut cold = Cell { material: Material::Snow, temperature: -2 };
    melt(&mut warm); melt(&mut cold);
    assert_eq!(warm.material, Material::Water);
    assert_eq!(cold.material, Material::Snow);
    assert_eq!(warm.temperature, 2);
}

03

Build a runoff scene

Put snow on a sloping roof and warm it. What should you measure?

Need a hint?

Conversion and later movement are separate observations.

Worked solution

Measure first Snow→Water conversion against 200 ticks at 1x. Then observe water leaving the slope and collecting below. Count Snow+Water in a sealed scene; do not accept a visual disappearance without water appearing.

When it goes wrong

Start with the observed symptom. Change one assumption at a time instead of pasting fixes until the error disappears.

Snow disappears with no puddle

What is happening: The melt branch erased instead of converting.

Try this: Assign Water in place and test combined volume.

Weather snow melts immediately in winter

What is happening: Creation temperature or precipitation selection is wrong.

Try this: Log surface ambient and initial Snow temperature.

Melted water hangs like powder

What is happening: A cached kind or marker was retained incorrectly.

Try this: Recompute kind from material and document timing of its next move.
