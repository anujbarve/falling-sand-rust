Exchange materials by density

Exchange sand, water and oil according to approved kind/density rules without passing through rigid solids.

Previously: Give materials properties and cells temperature. Keep that working result available before starting this lesson.

Why we need this now

The air-only prototype cannot show sand sinking in water or oil floating above it. Density exchange extends movement while preserving both materials. But a numeric density comparison alone is insufficient: it must also respect material kind and structural rules.

Your result: Exchange sand, water and oil according to approved kind/density rules without passing through rigid solids.

01Describe the intended exchange

If sand lies above water, the approved powder/fluid rule can move sand down and displace water upward. If oil lies above water, the lighter oil should not sink by the same downward exchange. A stable layered fixture makes those expectations explicit before code.

Use a consistent numeric scale for density values. The worked example uses small integer-relative values; the absolute unit is irrelevant to the ordering, but comparing quantities from inconsistent scales would be a bug. The material table and property lookup should define that convention once.

02Constrain which kinds may exchange

A dense stone cell should not automatically pass through another rigid solid or ignore its support group. Eligibility depends on kind and the relevant movement rule as well as density. Our focused function allows a moving powder/liquid to exchange only into air or eligible liquid under the chosen ordering.

That contract is a teaching baseline, not a complete buoyancy solver. Rigid-group flotation is handled later at group level. Keep separate systems separate: a property lookup can supply density, but it does not decide every motion consequence by itself.

03Preserve the paired state transfer

When an exchange is eligible, swap the complete cell state, not just material identity if temperature/moisture belong to the matter. Moving Sand identity while leaving its temperature behind would teleport heat between materials. Timers, progress and moved markers need equally explicit transfer semantics.

The worked fixture swaps enum values to isolate eligibility. Integrate it by moving the production Cell state consistently and marking the destination as updated. For a longer downward path, test each traversed location and apply a defined sequence; do not inspect only the final cell and leap through a wall.

04Test both motion and stability

Build closed columns for sand-over-water, oil-over-water and water-over-oil. Run the update enough times to see the selected ordering, then verify it remains stable under the same rule. Count each material before and after. A stable image without counts can hide duplication; preserved counts without expected ordering can hide a blocked exchange.

Also test Stone as an intervening blocker. That is the counterexample that prevents a naive “greater density always swaps” rule from taking over structural physics. The course’s goal is visibly consistent game behavior, not an unqualified claim of realistic fluid simulation.

#[derive(Clone,Copy,Debug,PartialEq,Eq)]
enum Material { Air, Oil, Water, Sand, Stone }
fn density(m:Material)->u16 {match m {Material::Air=>0,Material::Oil=>800,
    Material::Water=>1000,Material::Sand=>1600,Material::Stone=>2600}}
fn may_sink(a:Material,b:Material)->bool {
    let moving=matches!(a,Material::Oil|Material::Water|Material::Sand);
    let displaced=matches!(b,Material::Air|Material::Oil|Material::Water);
    moving && displaced && density(a)>density(b)
}
fn main() {
    assert!(may_sink(Material::Sand,Material::Water));
    assert!(!may_sink(Material::Oil,Material::Water));
    assert!(may_sink(Material::Water,Material::Oil));
    assert!(!may_sink(Material::Sand,Material::Stone));
    let mut pair=[Material::Sand,Material::Water];
    if may_sink(pair[0],pair[1]) {pair.swap(0,1);}
    println!("{pair:?}");
}

Read what each part does

  1. The helper is a complete eligibility fixture, not a full fluid solver.
  2. matches! names the accepted alternatives concisely.
  3. The actual Cell transfer must include more state than the enum-only demonstration.

03

Distinguish a raft

Should this helper decide a multi-cell wooden raft’s flotation?

Need a hint?

A rigid structure has group-level geometry and support.

Worked solution

No. The group flotation rule later considers the whole rigid group and liquid support. Reusing density values is reasonable; pretending a cell-exchange helper solves group buoyancy is not.

When it goes wrong

Start with the observed symptom. Change one assumption at a time instead of pasting fixes until the error disappears.

Stone sinks through other solids

What is happening: The rule compares only numeric density.

Try this: Constrain eligible kinds and retain support-group semantics.

Temperatures stay at old locations

What is happening: Only identities were swapped.

Try this: Transfer the complete matter-owned state under the approved contract.

Oil sinks below water

What is happening: Density ordering or exchange direction is reversed.

Try this: Check the pair fixture before altering global flow rates.

Explain it without the example

Why is density alone insufficient to decide whether every pair can swap?

Reasoning to compare

Kind and support rules constrain movement too. A dense cell must not arbitrarily pass through a rigid solid merely because its numeric density is greater.
