Connect seasons daylight and temperature

Season boundaries align with the integer clock. Visual side effects of accelerated climate are documented and tested.

Previously: Grow trees and cycle leaves. Keep that working result available before starting this lesson.

Why we need this now

A season is not only a label in the HUD. It changes sky colour, ambient temperature and ecological rates, while local matter responds through its own tick-based rules. Connecting those layers gives the calm world its rhythm, but it also exposes the deliberate difference between calendar speed and physical response.

Your result: Season boundaries align with the integer clock. Visual side effects of accelerated climate are documented and tested.

01Derive presentation from a single calendar

Use world_tick for time of day and seasonal position, then evaluate sky keys, sun path and climate parameters from that value. Do not let the renderer maintain a separate drifting day timer. A paused world should hold its sky and season. The night/dusk/day colours come from approved art keys; missing transition colours and moon-phase paths are visual decisions to approve. Interpolation should move coherently between those keys without randomly tinting neighbouring cells differently.

02Keep ambient as a world function

Ambient depends on season, time of day, weather-column offset and altitude relative to sea level. Evaluate it for the relevant cell or receiver, never the view’s top row. Sky-exposed and air-exposed conditions still have separate roles. Sheltered air uses the chosen season-mean policy instead of the full day/night swing. A basement can therefore remain more stable than an exposed puddle. This is a deliberately cheap game model, not a fluid simulation of air circulation.

03Let material respond through heat and moisture rules

Changing the season should not directly replace every Water cell with Ice or set all wood to dry. The ambient target changes, then exposure and heat exchange move cell temperatures; slow drying/growth opportunities use the new environmental context. This preserves causal links: shallow water freezes from its surface and sheltered wood can retain moisture. Use a fixture with exposed and sheltered copies so a direct global overwrite becomes obvious.

04Name the high-speed mismatch

At a large multiplier, the sky and ambient target can advance many calendar units while only one physical heat/movement update occurs. A winter may arrive before a deep lake has had normal-speed-equivalent cooling. That is an accepted fast-forward approximation if its visual outcome meets the milestone requirements. Record multiplier sequences and compare visible results at 1x,16x,64x.

fn ambient(base:i16, weather:i16, altitude:i32, sea_level:i32)->i32 {
    i32::from(base)+i32::from(weather)-(altitude-sea_level).div_euclid(100)
}
#[test]
fn climate_uses_world_altitude_not_camera_height() {
    assert_eq!(ambient(20,-2,0,0),18);
    assert_eq!(ambient(20,-2,200,0),16);
    assert_eq!(ambient(20,-2,-100,0),19);
}

How to verify your result

02

Keep shelter meaningful

What should differ between two identical wood cells, one outside and one under a roof?

Need a hint?

Ambient target and exchange eligibility are separate inputs.

Worked solution

The exposed cell receives direct weather/sun and its heat/drying context. The sheltered cell follows sheltered air and slower drying. They should not both be overwritten to one global moisture/temperature value.

How to verify your result

The paired fixture diverges for an explained exposure reason.

03

Explain a fast-forward pond

At the same world date, why might a capped run have less ice?

Need a hint?

Count physical heat updates before that date.

Worked solution

The capped run may execute fewer heat exchanges while advancing calendar time with larger multipliers. That is the model’s explicit approximation and requires a visibility/balance check, not a false equality assertion.

How to verify your result

The report includes both date and executed step count.

When it goes wrong

Start with the observed symptom. Change one assumption at a time instead of pasting fixes until the error disappears.

Sky moves while paused

What is happening: Rendering uses wall-clock time.

Try this: Drive presentation from paused world_tick.

All water freezes instantly at season change

What is happening: Climate code rewrites material directly.

Try this: Change ambient targets and let local thermal transitions act.

High-speed climate looks wrong

What is happening: Large calendar jumps exceed the chosen physical response behavior.

Try this: Test multiplier cases and tune/limit the accepted approximation.

Explain it without the example

Why can identical calendar dates produce different water states at different fast-forward limits?

Reasoning to compare

Different numbers of physical updates occurred before that date. The design deliberately separates physics progress from calendar advancement.
