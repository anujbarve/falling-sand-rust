Why we need this now

The first lava-water contact is a signature cause-and-effect scene for this sandbox. The player should see steam and cooled rock quickly. The mechanism joins existing heat transfer, material conversion and occupancy rules; the challenge is to make that join explicit rather than hide a collection of arbitrary pairwise hacks.

01Separate transfer from reaction selection

First determine whether the cells are neighbours and what heat moves across their shared edge. Then evaluate material transition rules using a clearly defined state: old temperatures or the completed heat-pass temperatures. Choose one pass order and test it. If some rules see old values and others see partially updated values, the first contact may depend on scan direction. The lab isolates reaction selection with already eligible temperatures; it is not a full physically accurate heat solver.

02Name the special contact policy

The design requires basalt at lava-water contact within one second. That is a visible game rule, not a consequence guaranteed by a minimal conductivity equation. Specify whether basalt forms from any contact or only after the lava cools below a threshold, then test that chosen rule. The example below adopts immediate eligible contact as an explicit teaching policy: Water becomes Steam and Lava becomes Basalt. Before production integration, compare that policy to the approved milestone decision.

03Preserve occupancy and clear stale state

Two occupied cells become two occupied cells. The water does not need a free air slot to become Steam because it changes in place; subsequent gas movement finds its path. Lava changes to a supported solid and must enter the support system. Do not place basalt into the water slot while also leaving the lava unchanged unless that is an explicitly budgeted mass source. Record which temperatures and flags result so repeated contact does not emit free extra steam every tick.

04Turn the deadline into a fixture

Place one lava cell next to one water cell, run ordinary simulation ticks, and record first Steam plus first Basalt. At 20 TPS the one-second deadline is 20 ticks at 1x. Compare that with a hot-sand/water fixture, whose maximum is three seconds. A test that merely checks the materials after thousands of ticks misses the actual promise. Keep rendering inspection too: a one-cell Steam change may be technically correct but visually unreadable without movement and colour.

#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq)]
enum Material { Water, Steam, Lava, Basalt }
fn eligible_contact(a: Material, b: Material) -> (Material, Material) {
    match (a,b) {
        (Material::Lava, Material::Water) => (Material::Basalt, Material::Steam),
        (Material::Water, Material::Lava) => (Material::Steam, Material::Basalt),
        _ => (a,b),
    }
}
#[test]
fn contact_is_symmetric_and_occupancy_preserving() {
    assert_eq!(eligible_contact(Material::Lava, Material::Water),
        (Material::Basalt, Material::Steam));
    assert_eq!(eligible_contact(Material::Water, Material::Lava),
        (Material::Steam, Material::Basalt));
}

01

Reverse the pair

Why does the test call the rule with both cell orders?

Need a hint?

A physical edge has no left-first identity.

Worked solution

The scan might discover either orientation. Reversed input must produce reversed output, not a different reaction. The two assertions catch a one-direction-only match.

How to verify your result

Left/right reversal preserves material outcomes.

02

Reject unrelated contact

Extend the enum with Stone and test it against Water.

Need a hint?

Use the unchanged default arm.

Worked solution

Add Stone and require the pair to remain Stone/Water. A special reaction should not turn every solid touching water into basalt or steam.

How to verify your result

An unrelated pair is unchanged.

03

Measure contact visibility

Write down the maximum engine ticks at 1x for lava-water and hot-sand-water.

Need a hint?

Base rate is 20 ticks per second.

Worked solution

Use at most 20 ticks for lava-water and 60 for hot-sand-water. Record the first eligible visible effect; do not hardcode the test to assume it waits until the limit.

How to verify your result

Both real-time requirements have explicit tick bounds.

When it goes wrong

Start with the observed symptom. Change one assumption at a time instead of pasting fixes until the error disappears.

Only water on one side boils

What is happening: Reaction selection omitted the reversed orientation.

Try this: Test both orderings through the same edge helper.

Every tick creates more basalt

What is happening: The original lava or contact flag survives after conversion.

Try this: Replace material atomically and inspect the next tick.

Unit rule passes but clip is slow

What is happening: Thermal eligibility or rendering delays the actual event.

Try this: Measure full fixture time rather than only calling the eligible helper.
