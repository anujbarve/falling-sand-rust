Restore grass after a fire

The isolated fixture either recovers under the approved rule or exposes a decision still needed. The seasonal recovery requirement is not claimed from neighbour spread alone.

Design choice to settle. The current specification leaves a decision here. The example makes the mechanism concrete; document the chosen behavior before treating it as the final game rule.

Why we need this now

A completely burned patch has no living grass neighbour. A neighbour-spread rule cannot recover it, no matter how many times we run the loop. That is a missing prerequisite, not a rate-tuning problem. We need to decide what survives or arrives after fire before promising recovery.

Your result: The isolated fixture either recovers under the approved rule or exposes a decision still needed. The seasonal recovery requirement is not claimed from neighbour spread alone.

01Trace the dependency of ordinary spread

The simple rule asks for Soil, sufficient light and moisture, and a nearby living Grass source. When all four exist, one eligible visit may replace a target with Grass. The newly created cell must not immediately spread again later in the same tick. This local rule is attractive because its cause is readable: green edges creep into bare soil. But it cannot manufacture its own starting source in a sealed all-burned fixture.

02Choose one minimal recovery mechanism

Options include surviving roots stored with soil, a bounded seed bank, rare seed arrival from the environment, or scoping the requirement to patches connected to surviving grass. Each changes behavior and storage. For this design lab, propose a boolean dormant-root condition that allows regrowth on exposed moist soil after fire. This is a teaching proposal, not an already approved material field. Compare its cost and player readability to a seed-source alternative before altering the packed Cell layout.

03Integrate death as well as growth

Grass buried under settled material loses light and eventually returns to soil under the current design. Fire can consume Grass into Ash, while leaves/ash on soil contribute soil formation. Regrowth should not appear through an intact stone floor or inside a dark cave merely because the seed/root condition exists. Put environment checks around the recovery source. A root mechanism may survive burning but not arbitrary erasure; decide and test those distinctions instead of allowing invisible immortal grass everywhere.

04Use the impossible case as the central test

Build a small isolated bare-soil island with no adjacent Grass. Under neighbour-only spread it should remain bare; that control proves the test is truly isolated. Enable the proposed root/seed source and run moist lit conditions through the seasonal deadline. Add dry and buried controls that must not recover. This demonstrates why the new mechanism is necessary and prevents “fixing” the requirement by accidentally leaving one hidden Grass cell in the fixture.

fn can_regrow(soil:bool, light:bool, moist:bool,
              grass_neighbour:bool, dormant_root:bool)->bool {
    soil && light && moist && (grass_neighbour || dormant_root)
}
#[test]
fn recovery_needs_a_source_and_good_conditions() {
    assert!(!can_regrow(true,true,true,false,false));
    assert!(can_regrow(true,true,true,false,true));
    assert!(!can_regrow(true,false,true,false,true));
    assert!(!can_regrow(true,true,false,false,true));
}

01

Prove isolation

How can you establish that the fixture has no ordinary spread source?

Need a hint?

Count and inspect all relevant neighbouring cells.

Worked solution

Assert zero Grass in the patch and its configured neighbourhood before starting. Run the neighbour-only control long enough to show no growth, then enable only the proposed source.

How to verify your result

The control remains bare without relying on a lucky random seed.

02

Respect a roof

Should the dormant root alone grow grass below an opaque floor?

Need a hint?

A source does not replace environment requirements.

Worked solution

No. The light/exposure predicate must still fail. Add a buried control and assert no growth until the covering material is removed.

How to verify your result

Dark/buried soil remains ungrown.

03

Review storage

Would adding a root bit be free in every representation?

Need a hint?

The eight-byte layout has a finite flags budget.

Worked solution

No. Audit used/spare flags and transition semantics. If the bit cannot fit or cannot be explained, propose a different source mechanism or revise the layout with measurements.

How to verify your result

The proposal states storage and conversion costs.

When it goes wrong

Start with the observed symptom. Change one assumption at a time instead of pasting fixes until the error disappears.

Burned islands never recover

What is happening: The only growth source was living neighbours.

Try this: Resolve the missing-source design decision explicitly.

Grass grows inside stone houses

What is happening: Recovery ignores soil/light conditions.

Try this: Require valid target material and exposure before applying the source.

A single tick greens the whole patch

What is happening: New targets spread immediately within the pass.

Try this: Stage edits or use correct full-tick eligibility metadata.

Explain it without the example

Why can't neighbour-only spread restore grass when no grass remains nearby?

Reasoning to compare

Its prerequisite is absent. Recovery needs a surviving source, a seed/root mechanism, or a revised scoped requirement.
