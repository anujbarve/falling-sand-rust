Distinguish sky exposure from air contact

Implement separate sky-exposure and air-contact queries so shelter and cooling do not contradict each other.

Previously: Exchange heat across an edge. Keep that working result available before starting this lesson.

Why we need this now

A roof should block rain while the room beneath it can still contain air. A cave wall should exchange heat with nearby air without being directly exposed to the sky. One generic exposed flag cannot answer both questions, so we will separate their geometry and test the difference.

Your result: Implement separate sky-exposure and air-contact queries so shelter and cooling do not contradict each other.

01Ask two different questions

Sky-exposed asks whether the cell is at the relevant top surface with an unobstructed direct-weather route under the game’s column model. Air-exposed asks whether at least one neighbouring location contains air. The first controls direct precipitation and sunlight; the second controls local heat exchange.

A cave wall can satisfy air contact while failing sky exposure. A roof’s top may be sky-exposed while its underside shelters the contents. These are not contradictory states; they describe different relationships. Naming them separately prevents a fix for rain from accidentally stopping all cave cooling.

02Define what the surface map includes

The current design’s surface heightmap counts settled blocking cells and ignores falling drops and plants for its base terrain surface semantics. Otherwise a raindrop would repeatedly become the highest non-gas cell and make terrain exposure flicker. Attached plant precipitation handling is a separate rule rather than an accidental heightmap side effect.

When a blocking cell is added above the current surface, update the column height. Removing the current surface requires finding the next valid blocker below. That search needs bounded storage/loading semantics in the vertically bounded world; do not assume a missing loaded chunk proves an unobstructed infinite column.

03Implement local contact independently

A four-neighbour air query checks the nearby cells that exchange heat under the chosen grid rule. It does not need to scan to the sky. At an active/storage boundary, use the explicit world-query policy rather than silently treating every absent allocation as ambient air.

The focused example uses a small hand-built roof and wall fixture, with a surface map derived from solid cells only. It prints the two predicates for a cell below the roof. Production will use the approved settled/material flags, but the central distinction remains the same: local contact and global column shelter are separate queries.

04Verify with counterexamples, not only open ground

Test an open surface, a roofed interior, a cave wall, a falling drop and a canopy. Confirm that drops do not rewrite the terrain surface and that shelter prevents direct precipitation without disabling all air contact. Include a roof edit so the cached surface map updates when geometry changes.

M1 can use fixed ambient inputs and handcrafted geometry; the live seasonal clock and procedural generator arrive later. That sequencing lets us prove the exposure contract before adding weather fields or large terrain. End the episode by tracing the same cell through a roof removal and explaining which predicate changes and why.

Download this worked example ↧

fn sky_exposed(x:usize,y:usize,surface:&[usize])->bool {surface[x]==y}
fn air_exposed(x:usize,y:usize,w:usize,h:usize,solid:&[bool])->bool {
    let neighbours=[(x as isize-1,y as isize),(x as isize+1,y as isize),
        (x as isize,y as isize-1),(x as isize,y as isize+1)];
    neighbours.into_iter().any(|(nx,ny)| nx>=0 && ny>=0
        && nx<w as isize && ny<h as isize && !solid[ny as usize*w+nx as usize])
}
fn main() {
    let (w,h)=(5,5);
    let mut solid=vec![false;w*h];
    solid[1*w+2]=true; // roof above this column
    solid[3*w+2]=true; // a wall cell below, beside local air
    let mut surface=vec![h;w];
    for x in 0..w {for y in 0..h {if solid[y*w+x] {surface[x]=y;break;}}}
    assert!(!sky_exposed(2,3,&surface));
    assert!(air_exposed(2,3,w,h,&solid));
    println!("wall: sheltered from sky, touching air");
}

Read what each part does

  1. The fixture deliberately separates geometry predicates from weather/temperature values.
  2. Missing neighbours are not automatically declared air here; production must define its boundary query policy.
  3. Surface-map inclusion and attached-plant rain handling are separate contracts.

Expected result

wall: sheltered from sky, touching air. This focused fixture uses downward rows; production altitude conversion is taught later.

01

Remove the roof

Clear the upper solid, rebuild the surface column and recheck the wall.

Need a hint?

The next blocker becomes the column’s surface.

Worked solution

The wall at row3 becomes the highest blocker in that column, so sky_exposed becomes true. Air contact can remain true. The two predicates need not change together.

How to verify your result

Cached geometry updates after the actual edit.

02

Add a falling drop conceptually

Should a moving raindrop above the roof redefine the settled terrain surface?

Need a hint?

The map’s inclusion rule is part of its contract.

Worked solution

No under the current settled-surface design. Precipitation and moving matter need their own handling; including every transient cell would make shelter tests unstable.

How to verify your result

You explain the membership rule rather than relying on gas/non-gas alone.

03

Test a sealed interior cell

Surround a solid cell with non-air neighbours while keeping it below a roof.

Need a hint?

Local air contact requires at least one actual air neighbour.

Worked solution

Both predicates can be false: it is sheltered and has no air-contact neighbour. Heat exchange with non-air neighbours remains a separate rule and should still run.

How to verify your result

The test does not equate lack of ambient contact with lack of all heat conduction.

When it goes wrong

Start with the observed symptom. Change one assumption at a time instead of pasting fixes until the error disappears.

Rain flickers between ground and drops

What is happening: Transient falling cells were included in the surface map.

Try this: Apply the settled/material inclusion contract explicitly.

Lava in a cave never cools

What is happening: Sky exposure was incorrectly used as the only air-contact test.

Try this: Use a separate local air-exposed predicate and sheltered ambient policy.

Roof removal leaves the room permanently sheltered

What is happening: The cached surface column was not updated.

Try this: Recompute the affected column under a bounded storage query and test edit invalidation.

Explain it without the example

Why must a cave wall be air-exposed but not sky-exposed?

Reasoning to compare

It touches air locally but has blocking terrain above. Heat contact and direct weather exposure answer different questions.
