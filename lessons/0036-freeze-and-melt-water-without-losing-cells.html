Freeze and melt water without losing cells

The pond freezes from the surface under the chosen heat rule and later melts. Total occupied water/ice volume is tracked.

Why we need this now

We already move water and transfer temperature. Now ask a concrete question: what should happen when the pond crosses zero? A colour change alone would not create an ice surface that supports a structure. The material identity has to change, but the world must still contain the same occupied volume. This lesson turns that requirement into one small, reversible state transition.

01Separate temperature from material

Think of a cell as a record containing both material and temperature. Two water cells may have different temperatures while sharing one material definition. Temperature determines whether the conversion is eligible; the material determines movement and support afterward. Do not encode “cold water” as a colour-only flag and expect the physics to infer that it became a solid. The conversion must explicitly replace Water with Ice. The renderer then reads that state rather than deciding it.

02Choose the boundary, including equality

The design says water below 0°C freezes and ice above 0°C melts. Therefore exactly zero does neither in this simple model. Write those inequalities before coding; changing < to <= changes the rule. A real physical phase-change model would account for latent heat and other details, but that is not what this game currently specifies. We are implementing the visible approximation and naming it honestly, then testing its boundary values.

03Transition once, then clear state deliberately

A conversion is an event, not a loop that repeatedly rewrites a cell. Read the old material and temperature, choose at most one replacement, then return. When porting into the packed Cell record, specify which flags survive. A water-flow marker should not accidentally make fresh ice move again, and a rooted plant flag should never appear on it. Preserve temperature unless the approved thermal rule says otherwise. Preserve or transform moisture according to its meaning; do not blindly reconstruct the default painted cell.

04From one cell to a pond surface

The helper proves thresholds and reversibility, not surface-first freezing. That larger behavior comes from exposure plus heat exchange: the upper water touches cold air, cools, becomes ice, and then conducts toward deeper water. Build a three-row pond fixture with cold air above and warm water below. Track the first converted row by tick and compare it with the ten-second deadline. If every row freezes simultaneously, look for a rule rewriting the entire pond to ambient temperature instead of letting heat move.

#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq)]
enum Material { Water, Ice }
fn phase(material: Material, temperature: i16) -> Material {
    match (material, temperature) {
        (Material::Water, t) if t < 0 => Material::Ice,
        (Material::Ice, t) if t > 0 => Material::Water,
        _ => material,
    }
}
#[test]
fn freezing_is_reversible_and_zero_is_stable() {
    assert_eq!(phase(Material::Water, -1), Material::Ice);
    assert_eq!(phase(Material::Ice, 1), Material::Water);
    assert_eq!(phase(Material::Water, 0), Material::Water);
    assert_eq!(phase(Material::Ice, 0), Material::Ice);
}

03

Prove volume in the world

Need a hint?

Count Water plus Ice, not Water alone.

Worked solution

Record the initial occupied-water-equivalent count. After either transition require water_count + ice_count == 20. Water alone should decrease on freezing; that is conversion, not loss.

How to verify your result

When it goes wrong

Start with the observed symptom. Change one assumption at a time instead of pasting fixes until the error disappears.

Water flips material every tick near zero

What is happening: Overlapping or equality-inclusive thresholds can alternate a zero cell.

Try this: Use mutually exclusive guards and test exact zero.

All pond rows freeze at once

What is happening: Ambient was assigned directly to every water cell.

Try this: Revisit air exposure and conduct heat inward instead.

Fresh ice still flows

What is happening: A movement path or cached flag ignores the new kind.

Try this: Derive movement from current material and reset transition-specific flags.

Explain it without the example

Why test the reverse transition instead of only freezing?

Reasoning to compare

One-way conversion can trap material permanently or leave stale flags. Reversal checks a complete cycle and transition cleanup.
