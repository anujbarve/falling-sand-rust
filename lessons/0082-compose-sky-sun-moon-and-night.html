Compose sky sun moon and night

Build a deterministic row-by-row sky ramp and place hard-edged celestial shapes without changing terrain.

Previously: Render through an art-pixel buffer. Keep that working result available before starting this lesson.

Design choice to settle. The current specification leaves a decision here. The example makes the mechanism concrete; document the chosen behavior before treating it as the final game rule.

Why we need this now

Atmosphere should come from colour, light discs and weather rather than noisy terrain detail. The sky is also an opportunity to separate view composition from world geometry so camera movement does not reshape the land.

Your result: Build a deterministic row-by-row sky ramp and place hard-edged celestial shapes without changing terrain.

01Make the gradient a calculation you can inspect

Choose two approved RGB anchors and a span in art rows. For a row inside the span compute its fractional distance, then interpolate each channel. Clamp above/below the ramp rather than extrapolating into unexpected colours. A three-key dusk ramp performs the same operation in two segments.

Use exact computed colours with no palette snapping or dithering. The examples’ named palette anchors come from the art spec; intermediate time-of-day keys still need approval. A smooth ramp does not require blur: every row can be a solid exact colour while geometry remains pixel-edged.

02Rasterize a disc without softened edges

A pixel belongs to a flat disc when its squared distance from the centre is no larger than the squared radius. This integer membership test draws hard boundaries. A halo ring is another flat disc behind it, not an opacity falloff. A crescent subtracts an offset disc from the lit disc within the approved construction.

Place these shapes in the view’s sky composition while allowing actual terrain to occlude them. Do not teleport the moon each time a ridge approaches it. The reference composition target describes a chosen camera view, not a rule overriding free-camera geometry.

03Decide where sky can exist

Air in a cave is not automatically open sky. Use the approved exposure/background treatment to keep the sky behind exterior air and the underground treatment behind sheltered cavities. This distinction must remain visible when the player excavates and builds.

Stars are sparse single art pixels in eligible night sky, with the explicitly allowed dusk-star exception. Do not fill every empty cell with stars. Sun/moon reflections, bloom, god rays and soft glow remain prohibited. Adding a weather cloud layer requires the separate approved treatment, not a new decorative terrain range.

04Connect time after the still frame works

First freeze world time and render the same frame repeatedly. Record camera, palette keys and visual time so comparisons are reproducible. Then interpolate approved sky keys using world_tick and position. Celestial paths/phases need explicit keys and occlusion tests at dawn, dusk and short/tall views.

The following terminal helper isolates ramp math and disc membership. Integrate it into your art image loop only after the endpoints and boundary pixels are correct. Floating point here is a colour interpolation tool, not a replacement for the integer world/calendar state.

fn ramp(top:[u8;3],bottom:[u8;3],row:u32,span:u32)->[u8;3] {
    assert!(span>0);
    let t=(row as f32/span as f32).clamp(0.0,1.0);
    std::array::from_fn(|i| (top[i] as f32+(bottom[i] as f32-top[i] as f32)*t).round() as u8)
}
fn in_disc(x:i32,y:i32,cx:i32,cy:i32,r:i32)->bool {
    let dx=i64::from(x)-i64::from(cx); let dy=i64::from(y)-i64::from(cy);
    dx*dx+dy*dy<=i64::from(r)*i64::from(r)
}
fn main() {
    let top=[15,26,51]; let horizon=[51,80,122];
    assert_eq!(ramp(top,horizon,0,400),top);
    assert_eq!(ramp(top,horizon,400,400),horizon);
    assert_eq!(ramp(top,horizon,900,400),horizon);
    assert!(in_disc(3,4,0,0,5));
    assert!(!in_disc(4,4,0,0,5));
    println!("sky endpoints and hard disc boundary verified");
}

Read what each part does

  1. The span must be positive; treating it as configuration with a validated minimum is better than hiding divide-by-zero behavior.
  2. The helper clamps beyond the horizon endpoint, so lower rows stay at that approved endpoint.
  3. Disc coordinates/radius in the actual art target are small; choose arithmetic bounds appropriate to production coordinate ranges.

01

Predict a middle row

Interpolate halfway between channel values 20 and 100.

Need a hint?

Halfway means t=0.5.

Worked solution

The channel is 60. Calculate all three channels independently; do not snap the result to a fixed palette.

How to verify your result

An assertion for the midpoint matches the helper.

02

Occlude the moon

A ridge rises across the moon while panning. What should change?

Need a hint?

Separate world geometry from celestial composition.

Worked solution

The ridge occludes the moon where appropriate. Keep the approved moon path; do not move the terrain or moon to protect a reference composition.

How to verify your result

A pan capture shows natural overlap without shape teleportation.

03

Choose the cave background

An empty underground room is drawn with the daylight sky. Which condition is missing?

Need a hint?

Air material alone does not prove sky access.

Worked solution

The background renderer must distinguish sheltered cavities from exterior sky-visible air according to the approved treatment. An underground view should not reveal stars through rock.

How to verify your result

The same world geometry renders a separate interior background.

When it goes wrong

Start with the observed symptom. Change one assumption at a time instead of pasting fixes until the error disappears.

Gradient bands appear after quantization

What is happening: Computed colours were snapped to a fixed palette.

Try this: Use the per-row calculated colour without palette snapping.

Sun edge is softened

What is happening: Canvas/graphics circle antialiasing or filtering was used.

Try this: Rasterize membership at the art grid and present nearest.

Night sky appears inside a cave

What is happening: Background choice checks only Material::Air.

Try this: Use the exterior/sheltered background rule rather than treating all air alike.

Explain it without the example

Why shouldn't a moon be moved each frame merely to avoid touching a mountain ridge?

Reasoning to compare

That would make composition override camera/world occlusion. The no-touch reference target is not a rule to teleport celestial objects during free panning.
