# Art style (approved mockup look; revision 2 integration draft)

Working name: TBD. A calm ecological sandbox. Pixel art, with the layering, light and haze of Alto's Odyssey. Atmosphere comes from flat light discs, smooth sky gradients and rain, not from texture or detail.

Companion: `2026-11-06-game-design.md`, revision 2.

The approved mockup look remains the visual baseline. **[rev 2]** marks reconciliation with the game design. **[proposed]** marks new visual decisions for review; these are not claims of prior approval. Unresolved choices are listed in section 12.

Precedence: the game design defines camera, simulation and v1 scope; this document defines their appearance. Explicit rules and scene exceptions here supersede obsolete mockup details. Reference descriptions do not silently override rules. A future change to either document must update conflicting companion rules.

---

## 1. Resolution and pixels

- **Reference art grid:** 960 x 540 art pixels for a 1920 x 1080 world view.
- **Display:** each art pixel is a 2 x 2 block of physical screen pixels. Scaling is nearest-neighbour; no smoothing or blur. DPI does not change this unit.
- **Edges:** hard pixel edges, never anti-aliased. Snap world drawing to the art grid after camera easing, so subpixel camera positions do not soften edges.
- **Resolution [rev 2]:** the world view uses the available physical pixels, subject to the machine's active-cell budget (game design sections 3.3 and 5.4). A larger view shows more world. Examples: 720p = 640 x 360 art pixels; 1080p = 960 x 540; 1440p = 1280 x 720; 4K = 1920 x 1080. These are sizes before any budget cap.
- **Cells [rev 2]:** one simulation cell is 2 x 2 art pixels, always 4 x 4 physical pixels. There is no zoom. Object dimensions measured in art pixels stay fixed relative to cells at every resolution; do not multiply tree, terrain or cell dimensions by grid height / 540.
- **Composition units:** sky-gradient spans, sky-object placement and illustrative horizon heights describe the reference frame. They are composition values, not world dimensions. Their handling in other views is specified in section 13. Particle counts describe density in the reference view, not fixed counts at every resolution.
- **Budget cap [rev 2]:** draw the world only inside the centred rectangle defined by the game design; remaining space uses the UI panel colour. UI placement is relative to that rectangle. **[proposed]** round its size down to whole cells and align its origin to physical pixel boundaries; leave any odd edge pixels in the surrounding panel.

## 2. Colour rules

- **Exact colours.** Each pixel takes the colour it is drawn with. Do not snap colours to a fixed palette: palette snapping produced visible banding in the sky and ground during the mockups, and the approved frames avoid it.
- **Gradients are smooth.** Sky, sea and dusk glows use a linear ramp between two or three colours, computed per art row. No dithering, no noise on top.
- **Materials are flat or one ramp.** Each material is one colour, or one ramp from a light top to a deep bottom. No speckle, no noise, no pattern.
- **Palette family:** muted. Warm sunset and dusk (peach, rose, violet). Night (navy, slate blue). Rain (slate, grey-green). Day (pale sky, warm sand).
- **Saturation in reference frames:** low on everything except the celestial light source, which is the only bright, pure-looking element. Gameplay fire, lava and event flashes need explicit colour exceptions (section 12).

## 3. Sky

- **Default:** a vertical gradient from a deep top colour to a lighter horizon colour.
- **Gradients used in approved scenes** (top colour, horizon colour, span in art rows):

| Scene | Top | Horizon | Span |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beach at dusk | `#33325e` (violet), mid `#5e5680` at 60% | `#c98f92` (rose) | 336 |
| Night (mountains, plains, sea) | `#0f1a33` | `#33507a` / `#3a5a84` | 380-400 |
| Desert at midday | `#5f9dcc` (blue) | `#f4e8cf` (pale haze) | 380 |
| Forest under rain | `#3b4a52` (slate) | `#8fa39c` (grey-green) | 420 |

- **Flat sky:** allowed when a scene is meant to be stark. The dunes at sunset used one flat colour (`#f2cfa8`). Use only when the user asks for a flat sky.
- **Stars:** single art pixels, sparse, in the upper sky only. Night scenes, plus the single dusk star in the beach reference.
- **Clouds [rev 2]:** required for cloudy weather, storms and the rain-cloud tool. They are a sky layer driven by weather-column cloud cover, not a material. Their shape and opacity remain open (section 12); do not treat their absence in the mockups as a gameplay prohibition.

## 4. Light sources

- **Sun:** a flat disc with one flat halo ring. Radius about 30-40 art px. Inner colour near white-cream (`#fffbe9`), halo a warmer cream (`#fff1c4`, or `#f8dfb4` at sunset).
- **Moon:** a crescent built from three discs (halo, lit disc and cutout). Base disc radius 36 in `#243a62` (dark halo colour), a bright disc radius 27 in `#e6ebf4`, then a dark disc radius 23 in `#243a62` offset up and right to cut the crescent.
- **Placement in reference frames:** sun low near the horizon at sunset, high at midday. Moon high, in the upper part of the sky, never touching a ridge. Gameplay occlusion and free-camera composition are covered in section 13.
- **Forbidden:** god rays, glow falloff around the light, reflections of the light on water.

## 5. Layering

- **One main ground.** The mockup composition uses one connected foreground terrain mass with sky behind it. **[rev 2]** In play, occupied cells define its actual shape: excavation, caves, overhangs, disconnected falling groups and structures must remain visible. Never fill empty cells below a decorative crest.
- **No decorative middle terrain layers** unless the user asks for them. Water, shore material, clouds and simulated objects are not extra terrain backdrops. The multi-layer dunes (Alto-style) were an earlier mockup; the later rule removed the middle layers.
- **Crest rim:** the top row of the ground is one art pixel brighter than the body. Use the explicit scene colours below; “1.5x lighter” is not a colour calculation. Other materials need a chosen rim colour, with a cool or warm tint matching the scene:

| Scene | Ground body | Crest rim |
|---|---|---|
| Night mountains | `#1b2a48` | `#5d7cac` |
| Night plains | `#1d3050` | `#5f80b0` |
| Desert at midday | `#e2b679` | `#fbe0a8` |
| Forest under rain | `#1f2e26` | `#4f6a5a` |
| Night at sea (sea body) | `#1e3a5c` to `#0a1628` | `#5f80b0` |

- **Crest shape (reference construction only):** a sum of sine waves. Use one long wave for the main rise and fall (wavelength about 150-200 art px), one short wave for breakup (about 40-70 px), and keep the amplitude low for plains, high for mountains.
- **Mountains (reference construction only; overrides the sine recipe):** tent-shaped peaks. Each peak is a line going down from its top at a slope of 0.75-0.95 art px per horizontal art px. The valley floor between peaks sits about 60 art px above the bottom.

**Gameplay terrain [rev 2]:** seeded generation and rest constraints in game design section 4.3 own terrain geometry. The sine and peak recipes describe the visual target; they must not reshape simulated terrain. **[proposed]** Apply the one-art-pixel crest rim only to upward-facing exposed terrain/water surfaces, using the surface heightmap. Do not outline every material cell or add rims to cave ceilings and interior boundaries. Caves and structures need the treatments listed in section 12.

## 6. Objects on the ground

- **Pines:** dark silhouettes standing on the crest. Height 14-36 art px (mountain scenes) or 26-95 px (forest). Width about 0.28 x height. Body `#0d1830` at night, `#14201a` in the forest, with one lighter edge `#34493e` on the lit side.
- **Trunks:** a few forest trees have a 6-px trunk below the crest.
- **Grass tufts:** plains only. 1-2 px wide stalks, 2-5 px tall, colour `#0f1b30`, placed along the crest.
- **Density:** sparse. A forest holds about 60 trees across 960 px. A mountain variant may hold about 26 pines at the foot; the approved night-mountain reference itself has none. Do not fill the ground with objects.
- **Shape:** every object is a plain silhouette. No outlines, no interior detail, no shading gradients.
- **Living plants [rev 2]:** trunks are wood cells; foliage is attached leaf cells, with evergreen needles as a variant. These silhouettes are targets for cell templates, not decorative trees painted over the simulation. Growth, burning, detachment and rigid-body falls must change the visible cells. Art-pixel details must stay within occupied cells and must not hide a removed support or invent collision geometry. Exact templates, autumn colours and leaf/litter treatment are open.

## 7. Water

- **Sea:** in the reference frame, one water body from the horizon to the bottom. In play, render only actual water cells, including pools, shore boundaries and player disturbances. The sea reference uses a smooth ramp from a teal-slate at the horizon (`#1e3a5c`) to a deep navy at the bottom (`#0a1628`) over about 170 art px.
- **Wave line [rev 2]:** a rendered surface ripple, never simulated waves or tides. The reference has a 2-art-pixel amplitude sine line and a one-art-pixel light rim (`#5f80b0`). **[proposed]** confine ripple colour to occupied surface-water cells; never draw water into air or obscure the actual collision boundary. Use it on the sea only; small pools retain their real cell boundary. Motion remains open.
- **No reflections.** No moon or sun reflection, no sparkle, no glitter, no light column on the water.

## 8. Weather

- **Rain:** the approved rain reference uses about 1400 thin diagonal streaks across the reference frame, each 5-13 art px long, leaning left by about 0.4 art px per row downward. **[rev 2.2]** These decorative streaks are the only visual representation of airborne falling rain; gameplay precipitation is applied directly at each column's surface cell by game design section 9.2 rather than spawning 2 x 2 water blocks in the air. The fixed lean describes the reference; wind-dependent motion remains open.
- **Colour:** two tones. Far streaks `#9fb3ab` at about 30% opacity. Near streaks (25% of the total) `#c3d3cc` at about 55% opacity. Near streaks are drawn last so they sit on top of the scene.
- **Rain on the ground:** the reference overlays streaks on the visible ground. **[proposed]** In play, clip decorative rain at the first non-gas surface in each world column, including roofs and water, and hide it in sheltered interiors. This deliberately supersedes the reference overlay where it would imply rain inside a house. Light/medium/heavy streak densities and impact visuals remain open.
- **Other weather [rev 2]:** snow, fog and storms are required by the game design; their visual treatments are not yet decided (section 12).

## 9. Particles

- **Dust motes (desert, dunes):** single pixels in pale warm colour (`#fff1d6`), about 90 per frame, in the upper half of the frame.
- **Stars:** single art pixels, sparse, pale blue-white (`#c9d6ee`). Night only, except the single dusk star in the beach reference.
- **Fireflies, pollen and embers:** not yet used in an approved scene.
- **Thrown material [rev 2]:** explosion particles are simulated cells rendered at the same cell size and material colour as the grid, not single-art-pixel dust. Their position is snapped for drawing only; the renderer does not change their physical trajectory. Falling support groups retain their constituent material shapes.

## 10. Reference scenes (approved look)

1. **Dunes at sunset** (Alto-style). Flat peach sky, sun low on the left. Five layers in this mockup; the later rule keeps one ground only.
2. **Night mountains.** Navy gradient, moon top-right, one big mountain range as the ground, no pines (foot too high).
3. **Plains at night.** Navy gradient, moon top-left, one low rolling plain with grass tufts on the crest.
4. **Desert at midday.** Blue sky fading to pale haze, bright sun high right, one wide sand dune field with a light rim.
5. **Night at sea.** Navy gradient, crescent moon in the middle, one sea band from the horizon down.
6. **Beach at dusk.** Violet-to-rose smooth sky, one star, one sand ground with a shore band, one sea band.
7. **Forest under rain.** Slate-to-grey-green sky, pine silhouettes on one dark wet ground, diagonal rain.

These descriptions record the approved compositions; they do not override the precedence rule at the top of this document. Explicit exceptions: dunes now use one ground mass; night mountains have no pines; beach dusk permits one star and adjoining sand, shore and water regions within the simulated foreground.

## 11. Not allowed

- Blurred or smeared pixels, or any filter that softens edges.
- Sun, moon or star reflections on water.
- Noise, speckle or texture on the ground or sky.
- Palette snapping, which bands gradients.
- Decorative middle terrain layers, unless the user asks.
- Cartoon or cute shapes: no rounded eyes, no bouncing, no outlines.
- God rays, lens flares, or bloom.
- Unspecified decorative cloud treatments; required weather clouds need the separate visual decision in section 12.

## 12. Decisions still open

These are visual decisions, not reasons to change already specified game mechanics. New proposals elsewhere in this revision also need review.

- **Reference assets:** attach the approved images with exact versions. Their absence blocks reliable image comparison.
- **Material palette [rev 2.2]:** base hex colours for all 21 v1 materials are now defined in game design section 7.2 for M0-M4 builds. Scene-specific time-of-day tinting and final visual tuning for M5 remain open.
- **State readability:** approve distinguishable wet/dry, living/dead, rotten, rusted, hot/cold and burning states. Wet soil must visibly lighten as it dries; rust and rotten wood must be distinguishable before removal. Distinguish water/ice, stone/basalt, smoke/steam and oil/water at day and night. Use flat colours or coherent ramps/variants; no random per-cell speckle. Whether temperature needs additional feedback remains open.
- **Structures and caves:** approve a house/bridge sample and an underground sample, covering interior backgrounds, wood/metal boundaries and silhouette readability without outlines or texture. No lamp lighting in v1.
- **Weather:** cloud shapes, storm darkness, snow, fog, weather-front blending and the visual relation between decorative rain and simulated drops. Fog must not introduce blurred geometry or hide required reactions.
- **Animation:** rain speed/density by weather intensity, wind response, ripple wavelength/speed, dust drift, star appearance, plant growth and leaf fall. Plant motion must reflect the simulation; decorative sway may not misrepresent occupied cells. Rain and other physics effects follow the physics rate; seasonal palette changes follow the world clock.
- **Celestial recipes:** halo thickness, crescent cutout offset, moon phases, sunrise/sunset key times and sky/material transition colours. The fixed crescent is a reference phase, not the only gameplay phase.
- **Emissive materials and events:** fire/lava colours, smoke/steam opacity, lightning and explosion-flash intensity/duration. Decide explicit exceptions to the muted-palette rule for these gameplay signals. Bloom, soft glow and light reflections remain forbidden. Source-cast lighting and lamps are outside v1.
- **UI:** font asset/licence, minimum readable physical text size, UI scale choices, panel/text/selection colours, material swatches, brush preview and selected tool feedback. UI may have its own integer scaling without changing world-cell size. Whether text must use the world art grid remains open. Panel colour is also needed for capped views.

## 13. Composition across views [proposed]

These defaults make the reference-frame measurements usable in the resizable, freely panned world. They are proposed visual rules, not previously approved mockup decisions.

- **World geometry:** terrain, objects, pools and their positions stay in world coordinates. A resize reveals or hides world; it does not stretch objects or move terrain to preserve a mockup horizon. Spawn/reference camera positions provide the intended starting composition.
- **Sky:** sky is drawn only behind air open to the sky, not through underground cavities. Treat the reference gradient spans as fractions of the 540-row reference view, scaled to the current world-view height; clamp outside the ramp. Use the world-view rectangle, excluding any budget panels. Underground background treatment is still open.
- **Sun and moon:** use view-relative sky positions driven by time, with the reference radius fixed in art pixels. They do not pan as nearby world objects. Foreground geometry occludes them. Do not move the moon to evade a ridge during free panning; “never touching a ridge” is a reference-composition target. Paths, phase transitions and visibility on very short views require approval.
- **Density:** decorative rain, stars and dust use reference counts per eligible art-pixel area: scale rain with exposed weather area and dust/stars with eligible sky area, then apply the graphics budget. Do not stretch streaks or enlarge particles on larger displays. Density caps and sparse-star count remain open.
- **Time and biome transitions:** interpolate sky/material colour keys coherently using world time and position. Never recolour cells independently with random noise. Exact keys and blend widths remain open. Time changes colours without reshaping world geometry.

**Water/material ramps:** the sea's 170-art-row ramp is a reference recipe. **[proposed]** anchor it to the water surface in world coordinates and keep its art-row depth fixed when resizing or panning. Do not anchor material shading to the bottom of the screen. Small pools and separated drops need their own approved flat colour or ramp rule (section 12).

## 14. Visual acceptance [rev 2]

- Use fixed seed, generator version, camera position, physical world-view size, world time, physics step and weather state for each reference capture. Freeze decorative animation at a recorded visual time as well. Keep UI visibility and graphics preset fixed.
- Capture the seven reference compositions at 1920 x 1080 when the machine budget permits. Separately inspect 720p, 1440p, a Retina/native view, a narrow/tall window and a capped view: cells remain 4 physical pixels; world geometry is unchanged; clipping, UI and sky composition remain readable. Higher resolutions are not expected to produce resized copies of the reference image.
- Add gameplay samples for excavation/caves, a shelter in rain, growing/burning trees, wet-to-dry soil, rust/rot, freezing water, and fire/lava against day and night palettes. A reaction must be recognisable within the companion design's visibility limits.
- Until image assets and allowable comparison tolerances are approved, visual checks are manual reviews; screenshot diffs may report differences but cannot establish approval. Record the final assets, capture conditions and tolerance policy before making them a release gate.
